# Prasad lens

Pulak Prasad, Nalanda Capital. Permanent owner of a high-return business. This lens refuses a forecast. It is more hostile to a turnaround than the Jhunjhunwala lens. When both ran, keep both.

Nalanda's page (https://www.nalandacapital.com/companies-need-to-qualify-a-rigorous-screening-process/) states four screens: a consistently high return on capital, including after a temporary decline; an attractive industry; a clean and outstanding entrepreneur; an asymmetric risk and reward. The page says the fourth screen uses financial modeling. Accounts of the book *What I Learned About Investing from Darwin* say he does not forecast and does not build a DCF. If both are in front of you, show both. If you did not build a model, write "model not built". That is not a fail of the business, and it is not a reason to invent a DCF.

Public summaries of the book put the steps in this order: avoid big risks, buy high quality at a fair price, then be very lazy. The risks named there are a promoter who cheats customers, suppliers, employees, or shareholders; a turnaround; a love of debt; a serial acquirer. Use those as the risk list. Do not add a zero-debt cutoff the page does not print.

## Iron rules

1. India only. NSE, BSE, or an India bootstrapped company. Anything else: stop and say so. This lens needs a listed return series. A private company without filings: say so and stop.
2. Every figure has source, period, and consolidated or standalone. If it is not in a source, write "not found". Do not estimate silently.
3. INR, with crore or lakh stated. Indian fiscal year (April–March) unless the filing says otherwise.
4. Separate fact, inference, and opinion.
5. Promoter holding, pledge, related parties, and auditor notes are part of the analysis.
6. Close with this disclaimer. No buy, sell, or target presented as advice or as a certain outcome.

> This is an educational research memo, not investment advice, not a SEBI-registered recommendation, and not a solicitation to buy or sell any security. Figures are only as good as the filings cited. You decide what to do with them.

Listed facts come from `../analyze-financials/SKILL.md` if they are not already on the page.

## Tests

1. Not a turnaround. If the thesis is that a failing business will recover, fail. A temporary drop in return on capital is allowed by the Nalanda page. Say which one you have.
2. Return series. ROCE from the financials skill for the years opened. The page says "consistently high" and prints no percent. Do not insert 15% or any other cutoff. One year is not the series.
3. Promoter. Pledge, related parties, auditor notes, and any SEBI order you opened. Not found is not a pass. "Clean" is not a pass from silence.
4. Debt and acquisitions. Report net debt and lease liabilities from the financials skill, and whether the filing shows a string of acquisitions. "Detest debt" is his preference. The opinion sits next to the figures. It is not a kill you invented.
5. Fair price. The sourced close and multiple, or "not found". No target. Do not forecast next year's earnings to justify the price.
6. Be very lazy. His description of holding a name that passed. It is not an instruction to the reader.

## Kill

Fail when the write-up needs a turnaround or a forecast of next year's profit. Do not fail a single down year that the page would call temporary, unless the thesis is that the business is being rescued.
