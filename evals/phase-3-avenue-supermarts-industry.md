## SWOT

### Strengths

1. Evidence: Consolidated revenue from operations ₹68,820.74 crore and operating margin 6.14% for the year ended 31 March 2026, against the only other listed row opened. Sources: Phase 2 annual-report figures, and the margin arithmetic above.
2. Evidence: 500 stores at 31 March 2026, Avenue directors' report.
3. Evidence: Revenue from customers entirely in India, Avenue note 54, as cited in the Phase 2 memo.

### Weaknesses

1. Evidence: Trade payables ₹1,338.23 crore against inventories ₹5,900.64 crore and receivables ₹149.34 crore, consolidated, 31 March 2026. Phase 2 memo. Payables do not fund inventory and receivables.
2. Evidence: Cash after the property, plant and equipment line was negative in both years opened in Phase 2. Maintenance was not split from new stores, so owner earnings stayed not found.
3. Evidence: Avenue E-Commerce loss ₹306.53 crore (prior ₹247.37 crore), directors' report in the Phase 2 memo. That figure is the subsidiary as the directors state it, not a line of consolidated profit.

### Opportunities

1. Hypothesis: More Indian households buy the defined staples basket in physical value supermarkets. No market-size source was opened.
2. Hypothesis: Quick commerce stays adjacent, and the staples basket remains in the stores already counted. No channel figure was opened.

### Threats

1. Hypothesis: Blinkit, Zepto, or Instamart takes the same household staples basket. Figures not found.
2. Hypothesis: A grocery format of Reliance Retail does the same. Figures not found.
3. Hypothesis: The labour codes notified on 21 November 2025 raise staff cost. Spencer's note 6 records ₹375.74 lakhs inside its own employee benefits for past service and leave. Avenue's amount for the same codes was not in the Phase 2 memo.

### Dropped for lack of evidence

- India grocery market size, and any share of it.
- "Clean promoter" as a strength. The pledge column was not opened.
- Vishal Mega Mart, V-Mart, and Trent as grocery peers.
- Quick-commerce revenue, and Reliance Retail grocery revenue.

## Critic

- Breaks the thesis: A sentence that Avenue Supermarts leads Indian grocery, or holds a stated share, would break. Market size is not found. Unlisted retailers were not sized. The memo does not contain that sentence. The two-row revenue comparison is sourced and is not a share.
- Weakens it: Spencer's segment is "organised retail". The results PDF does not split food from other goods, so the peer row may be wider than the market definition. Only one listed peer was opened. Spencer's promoter holding, pledge, and related parties were not in the results PDF. Avenue's pledge was not re-opened. Store direction is one period.
- Nit: Spencer's ₹1,800.00 crore is 1,79,999.83 ₹ lakhs divided by 100 and rounded to two decimals.

Checked: both revenue rows are consolidated for the year ended 31 March 2026. Quarter columns were not used. No global market size is used. The leader section does not paste the peer table. No sentence tells the reader to buy, sell, or expect a price.

## Revised conclusion

Do not conclude who leads this market. Among the two listed rows whose FY 2025-26 revenue was opened, Avenue Supermarts has the larger consolidated revenue, a positive operating margin on the definition above, and 500 stores at 31 March 2026. Share is not found. Spencer's consolidated equity is negative and the results carry a going-concern note. That is a fact about Spencer's accounts. It is not a measure of the Indian grocery market.

## What would change the conclusion

A cited India grocery size, with the publisher, period, and boundary, from a page that was opened. Spencer's own split of food and grocery inside "organised retail", or a second listed grocery filer opened on the same basis. A second period of Avenue store count from a filing. Sized figures for Reliance Retail grocery or a named quick-commerce operator, from their own pages.
