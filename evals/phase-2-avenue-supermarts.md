# Avenue Supermarts — research memo

Phase 2 dry run. One listed company, one annual report, then Damani, Buffett, Munger, and the critic. Industry skills were not run.

## Business

Avenue Supermarts runs organised retail in India and operates supermarkets under the name D-Mart. The consolidated accounts are one segment: the managing director looks at the company as a whole. Goods are transferred at a point in time. Note 54 says revenue from customers is entirely in India and nil outside India. The directors' report, which is other information and not the auditor's opinion on the accounts, says the company added 85 stores in the year and had 500 stores on 31 March 2026, across 15 states, one union territory, and the NCR, and that it prices on an everyday-low-cost, everyday-low-price principle. A subsidiary, Avenue E-Commerce Limited, runs online and multi-channel grocery under DMart Ready.

### How the computed figures were built

Consolidated, unless the sentence says standalone. EBIT leaves no exceptional line out, because the profit and loss presents none: profit before tax plus finance cost. FY26 EBIT 4,223.67. FY25 EBIT 3,742.12.

Cost base for days = purchases plus the inventory-change line. FY26: 59,315.99 − 856.26 = 58,459.73. FY25: 51,668.76 − 1,117.06 = 50,551.70.

- ROE FY26 = 2,970.49 / average of 24,464.04 and 21,427.75 = 12.9%. ROE FY25 = 2,708.02 / average of 21,427.75 and 18,697.82 = 13.5%.
- Capital employed = owners' equity + borrowings − cash. Leases are not in this denominator. FY26 ending 25,145.60. FY25 ending 21,072.27, with borrowings nil. FY24 ending 18,360.70, using cash-flow opening cash of 337.12 and nil borrowings, because the comparative financing lines for borrowings are dashes and note 22 opens the next year from a dash.
- ROCE FY26 = 4,223.67 / average of 25,145.60 and 21,072.27 = 18.3%. ROCE FY25 = 3,742.12 / average of 21,072.27 and 18,360.70 = 19.0%.
- Interest cover = EBIT / finance cost = 29.7 times and 53.9 times. Lease interest is 85% and 97% of that finance cost.
- Net debt = borrowings − cash. Other bank balances are not subtracted. FY26: 965.27 − 283.71 = 681.56. FY25: nil − 355.48 = net cash 355.48. Lease liabilities of 1,459.72 and 819.62 are reported beside this and are not inside it.
- CFO / PAT = 3,466.74 / 2,969.86 = 1.17, and 2,462.97 / 2,707.45 = 0.91. PAT here is profit for the year, not the attributable line.
- Other income / profit before tax = 1.8% and 3.4%.
- Inventory days = 5,900.64 / 58,459.73 × 365 = 36.8 (prior 36.4). Payable days = 8.4 (prior 7.7). Receivable days = 149.34 / 68,820.74 × 365 = 0.8 (prior 0.9).
- Inventory plus receivables is 6,049.98. Payables are 1,338.23. Payables do not fund inventory. The sign is not inferred from grocery retail.
- Cash from operations minus the PPE line = 3,466.74 − 4,113.35 = (646.61), and prior (960.07). This is cash after the whole capex line, not free cash flow after maintenance.
- Standalone Align purchases 3,841.31 / standalone revenue 66,968.03 = 5.7%. Standalone sales to Avenue E-Commerce 2,574.39 / 66,968.03 = 3.8%.

## Statements

Profit rose. Consolidated profit for the year went from 2,707.45 to 2,969.86. Cash from operations went from 2,462.97 to 3,466.74. The PPE line was larger than cash from operations in both years.

The balance sheet at 31 March 2025 shows borrowings as a dash. Note 22 prints a dash on every prior-year borrowing line, and the cash-flow reconciliation opens borrowings at 1 April 2025 on a dash. That dash is read as nil. It is not an unread cell.

Closing cash on the cash-flow statement is "(96.56), net of bank overdrawn balances". Balance-sheet cash is 283.71. Overdraft in note 22 is 380.27. The bridge is 283.71 − 380.27 = (96.56). The net-change line's text shows 452.04 without brackets. Read as an increase, it does not reach (96.56). The operating, investing, and financing subtotals do. Those subtotals are what this memo uses.

CFO / PAT was below 1 in FY25 and above 1 in FY26, while profit rose. The multi-year flag does not fire. Other income is 1.8% of profit before tax, against 3.4% the year before. This memo does not call that large. Interest cover is 29.7 times. The finance-cost flag does not fire. Most of the finance cost is lease interest, and lease liabilities are larger than borrowings.

The company's own standalone debt-equity ratio is 0.09, and the note includes lease liabilities in the numerator. That ratio is not consolidated net debt.

## Promoter, pledge, related parties, auditor

Promoter holding is in note 53 of this annual report. Radhakishan Damani holds 14,98,48,238 shares, 22.98%. The promoter and promoter group hold 48,57,96,256 shares, which is 74.51% of the shares in the share-capital note. Pledge of those shares is not found. The Regulation 31 column was not opened. Note 36 pledges the company's receivables and inventories for borrowings. That is not a promoter pledge.

Related parties on the consolidated note: PPE bought from Mrs Kirandevi Damani for 25.81, and goods bought from Palya Footwear for 39.73. Consolidated related-party trade payables are 0.35. Standalone purchases from Align Retail, 3,841.31, and standalone sales to Avenue E-Commerce, 2,574.39, are transactions with subsidiaries. They are not added again onto the consolidated totals.

The auditor's opinion is unmodified. Two subsidiaries have a CARO paragraph 3(xvii) remark. That paragraph asks about cash losses. The annexure does not give the amounts. The directors' report says Avenue E-Commerce lost 306.53 on revenue of 4,093.61, and Reflect Healthcare lost 4.90. A loss in the directors' report is not the cash-loss figure the CARO clause asks for. Key audit matters are new-store capital expenditure and inventory existence. The inventory matter cites a carrying amount of 5,900.64, an allowance of 70.08, and shrinkage and damage of 275.24 recorded in the year.

No dividend was paid. 12,26,055 shares were issued on exercise of options.

## Flags

No flag in the statement rules fires on the years opened.

CFO / PAT was below 1 in only one of the two years. Other income is a small share of profit before tax on the figures above. Pledge was not disclosed in a filing this memo opened, so it is not "above zero" and it is not zero. The auditor did not qualify the consolidated opinion. The key audit matters are capex and inventory, not a related-party or revenue qualification. Finance cost at 29.7 times cover does not consume a large share of EBIT. Lease liabilities are still the larger obligation, and they sit outside net debt.

## Lens: Damani

- Build it: Not a fail. The product is simple on the filing: Indian supermarkets, goods sold at a point in time, all of the revenue in India. Repeat demand is an inference from that format. The filing has no cohort.
- Multiple versus price: Not found. Cash after the PPE line is negative in both years, capital commitments for sites under construction are 5,294.10, and no note splits maintenance from new stores. This memo does not assign a multiple, and it does not copy a peer median. No exchange price was opened, so there is no comparison with price.
- Lend to the promoter: Not found. Note 53 gives the holding. The pledge column was not opened. SEBI orders were not searched. An unmodified audit does not turn a missing pledge record into a pass.
- Debt and working capital: Fail on working capital. Inventory plus receivables, 6,049.98, exceed payables, 1,338.23. A grocery format is not evidence the other way. Borrowings are 965.27 against owners' equity of 24,464.04, and FY25 borrowings are a dash read as nil. Net debt on the borrowings definition is 681.56. Lease liabilities are 1,459.72, larger than borrowings, and are not inside that net debt. Interest cover is 29.7 times. "High debt" is not the finding. "Little or no debt" is unavailable once the lease liability is on the page.
- Cash flow and capex: Fail for these two years on cash after the capex line: (646.61) and (960.07). Maintenance capex is not found. The auditor's key audit matter describes land and buildings for new stores. The directors' report says 85 stores were added. New stores are not maintenance. The PPE line, 4,113.35, is larger than depreciation, 1,037.02.
- Government discretion: Not what protects the profit on the pages opened. The accounts describe supermarkets, store construction, and leases. No tariff or tender is cited as the source of profit.
- Core versus trading (only if asked): Not asked.
- India caveat: Ind AS, April–March, consolidated rupee crore. Sampat's cash-and-capex test is this test 4 and test 5, not a separate lens.
- Refuses to opine on: any multiple, any price, and whether the promoter can be lent to.

## Lens: Buffett

- Understandable: Pass. Indian supermarket retail under D-Mart, goods sold at a point in time, revenue in India.
- Moat: Not found as a finished test. Owned property, plant and equipment of 17,586.95, right-of-use assets of 2,381.41, and 500 stores in the directors' report are facts about the asset base. Share against a named rival was not opened. A slogan without that comparison is not a moat.
- Management: Not a pass. Holding is in note 53. Pledge is not found. Related-party PPE of 25.81 from a promoter, and Palya purchases of 39.73, are facts, not a failed character test by themselves. The audit opinion is unmodified. No dividend was paid, and the directors tie that to expansion. Capex is the new-store programme above, and cash after that capex is negative. Missing pledge is not a pass.
- Reinvestment return: Not a pass. Two years were built from this filing. ROE 12.9% and 13.5%. ROCE 18.3% and 19.0% on equity plus borrowings minus cash, with leases left out of the denominator. Cash after the PPE line is negative in both years. FY24 as its own annual report was not opened.
- Price versus owner earnings: Owner earnings not found. The maintenance piece cannot be split. Facts left on the table: profit for the year 2,969.86, depreciation and amortisation 1,037.02, PPE line 4,113.35. New stores, and a capex line larger than depreciation, are not maintenance. Working capital consumed cash: the cash-flow inventory increase is 856.27. Price was not opened, so price against earnings is not found. Basic EPS of 45.65 is a fact from the profit and loss. It is not a target.
- India caveat: Ind AS. Promoter control is the normal Indian structure. Honesty is the filing record above, not an imported partnership standard.
- Refuses to opine on: owner earnings as one number, and whether the price is cheap.

## Lens: Munger

- Paths to failure: Three, each an inference tied to a fact in this report. First, store building can keep absorbing cash. Cash from operations was 3,466.74 and the PPE line was 4,113.35, while commitments for sites under construction were 5,294.10 and the directors' report added 85 stores. Second, DMart Ready can keep losing money beside the stores. The directors' report shows Avenue E-Commerce revenue up from 3,502.42 to 4,093.61 and the loss wider, from 247.37 to 306.53, and the auditor lists CARO paragraph 3(xvii) for that subsidiary without stating the cash-loss amount. Third, the lease and borrowing stack can keep rising with the stores. Lease liabilities went from 819.62 to 1,459.72, borrowings went from a dash to 965.27, and finance cost went from 69.45 to 142.05, of which 120.76 is lease interest. Interest cover is still 29.7 times, so this path is not a claim that the cover has already failed.
- Bias in the bull case: No bull case is written in this memo. The test is skipped. A bull case is not invented in order to attack it.
- Several forces or one ratio: Several facts. Cash after capex, the lease stack, the subsidiary loss, and working capital that is not funded by payables. The memo does not rest on one multiple.
- Quality at a fair price: Not found. Price was not opened. The cash-versus-profit flag did not fire across both years. The moat test was not finished. A low multiple on a weak business is not something this memo can score.
- India caveat: The failure paths are Indian filings: store capex, a grocery e-commerce subsidiary, and Ind AS 116 leases. No path uses a global peer multiple.
- Refuses to opine on: a price target, and a portfolio action.

## Where the lenses disagree

Damani does not pass. Working capital fails on the figures, cash after the capex line fails for both years, the multiple is not found, and lending to the promoter is not found.

Buffett can explain the business. Owner earnings are not found, management is not a pass, and the moat is not finished.

Munger states three ways the business can go wrong and does not kill the case. Nothing here depends on a forecast of next year's multiple.

These are left as three results. They are not averaged into one view.

## Critic

- Breaks the thesis: There is no positive thesis in the memo. One that said the Damani lens passes because the promoter is Radhakishan Damani, or because the company runs grocery stores, would break. Tests on the multiple, the pledge, working capital, and cash after capex do not pass. The 500 stores and the 22.98% holding are facts. They are not those tests.
- Weakens it: The Regulation 31 pledge column was not opened. SEBI orders were not searched. No NSE or BSE price was opened. Maintenance capex is not split. The CARO paragraph 3(xvii) amounts are not in the annexure. The capital-work-in-progress ageing table did not extract, so overdue projects are not found. Only two statement years are in this report. A later pass would still be a hypothesis until those are opened.
- Nit: Note 29's inventory movement is ₹0.01 crore away from the cash-flow inventory line. The cash-flow "452.04" has no brackets in the text layer. The memo uses the bridge that reaches (96.56), not the unsigned figure as an increase.

Checked: consolidated and standalone are in separate rows; Align and Avenue E-Commerce trading is not folded into a consolidated ratio; other income and CFO versus PAT are stated; the lenses disagree in the open; no sentence tells the reader to buy, sell, or expect a price.

## Revised conclusion

Do not conclude. The business is an Indian supermarket chain, and that is as far as the understandable-business test goes. Cash after store capex is negative, payables do not fund the inventory, owner earnings cannot be built until maintenance is split out, and the promoter pledge was not opened. A price was not taken from the exchange. None of that is a target, and none of it is a reason to do the opposite.

## What would change the conclusion

A capex note that splits maintenance from new stores, so owner earnings can be built. The Regulation 31 pledge column. A search of SEBI orders on the company and the promoters. An NSE or BSE price on a named date, and a multiple justified from this company's cash rather than from a peer median. The amounts behind the two CARO paragraph 3(xvii) remarks. A readable ageing of capital work-in-progress.
